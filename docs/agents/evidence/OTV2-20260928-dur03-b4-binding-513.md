The example builds the decision's upper-bound construction: every bounded field at its registered
maximum, every varint at its widest, in the normative `EventEnvelope` with every optional field present.
The measured values equal the decision exactly:

| Case | Payload | Envelope | Overhead |
|---|---|---|---|
| MINT | 6,129 B | 7,167 B | 1,038 B |
| TRANSFER, `typed_position` excluded | 7,433 B | 8,471 B | 1,038 B |
| TRANSFER, `typed_position` 128 B (test-only wire) | 7,565 B | (8,603 B by arithmetic) | — |

The byte gates accept the 7,167 B MINT worst case. Field-bounded input cannot reach the caps, so the
size gates are exercised directly at 9,216/9,217 B and 7,936/7,937 B before decode. Per-field max+1
(513 B content key, 129 B technical field, 15/17 B UUID, 31/33 B digest) is rejected with
`INVALID_INPUT`, and nothing truncated is produced.

## Findings

- The decision's 1,038 B overhead is reproduced only when the two envelope enums are encoded at their
  widest positive int32 (5 B) and the `TransactionEventRef` ordinal/count at their widest u32. That
  byte-bound probe is therefore rejected by the RL-07 one-event row (`count` must be 1). Every
  semantically admissible envelope is smaller than 7,167 B, so this is conservative and not material.
- The event type id `2` is the next unused id under the registry's `ids_never_reused` rule. The
  decision's handback requires registering the event type, but it gives no numeric id.
- The `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` versus `INVALID_INPUT` choice per row follows the decision's §3.2 failure line:
  count/byte-size rows use `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, and per-field width and excluded shapes use `INVALID_INPUT`.
- `NOT_PROVEN`: runtime, SQL, outbox or restart authority; RL-08 under real PostgreSQL (stage C);
  death-key construction and descendant fences (stage D); TRANSFER admission; production collection.
