## 0. How to read and approve

- **Family** is the A12/WO-0 family: `Item` (key `oteryn:item.tibia.i<id>`, A12 D146) or `WorldObject` (key `oteryn:world-object.tibia.i<id>`, A12 §4.6; the Item entry then stays a non-materializable `routed_to` pointer). `Terrain` was not needed for any of the 266 ids.
- **Profile** is the item-authoring lane's own `family_profile` (the 22 ids of `profile-catalog.json`), which is what the earlier 121-id owner table (`owner-item-family-decisions.json`) decided. `Item` rows need one; `WorldObject` rows do not.
- **Key.** Every one of the 266 ids is a CipSoft appearance object id in the client 15.30 file, so each has a *current* key under A12 with no exceptions (A12 §4.1). The provisional `client:tibia@15.30-2dfa943b:item/<id>` keys (list A) and the epoch-2 / registry keys (lists B and C, shown per row) are retired to aliases by ITEM-ID-1.
- **Confidence.** HIGH = the wiki page for this exact itemid gives an admitted primarytype, or its notes name a specific quest role. MEDIUM = same-named wiki page for other ids of the item, an explicit quest/event link found for the item, or an existing lane rule. LOW = only a weak analogy. Approve HIGH groups as a whole; MEDIUM groups can go through unless you object to the alternative named in the row; look at the LOW rows individually.
- Wiki mapping uses the lane's own admitted table (`engine_items.PRIMARYTYPE_PROFILE` via `resolve_wiki_family_value`), so a wiki primarytype of 'Quest Items' always means `quest_item`, 'Fansite Items' means `event_collectible`, 'Soul Cores' means `material_valuable`, and so on. No new mapping is introduced.
