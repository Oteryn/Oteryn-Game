## Outcome

- **Step-speed table.** `tools/content-schema/step-speed` generates, offline, the u16 step speed of
  every clamped speed 10..=65,535 with Canary's `floor(857.36 × ln(speed + 261.29) − 4795.01 + 0.5)`
  into `content/movement/step_speed_v1.json`, with `sha256_u16le`. The server verifies the digest at
  load; the server and the client read the table and never evaluate `ln`. Tests pin the digest and
  Canary samples, and show no value sits within 1e-7 of a rounding boundary.
- **Effective speed and step duration.** `110 + level − 1 + SPEED delta + equipment term`, clamped to
  [10, 65,535]; `floor(1000 × ground / step speed)` rounded up to 50 ms. Ground speed comes from the
  §1.11 seam (`GroundSpeedSource`); production uses the engineering source, 150 on every tile. A
  ground speed of 0 refuses the step before anything moves. Only cardinal steps exist, so no × 3.
  Production reads the admitted actor's active `SPEED` condition delta from the Channel runtime's
  condition owner at the owner time; with no Character progression owner it uses level 1 (550 ms
  with no condition) until that composes.
- **Pacing.** Each connection keeps a pacing clock and a one-step buffer. An early step waits, without
  a result, until the previous moved step's duration has passed, then runs through the ordinary path.
  Frames read while it waits are held and run in CommandId order after its result (FND-02 §13); a
  step among them is refused: `TOO_EARLY` (4) under capability 13 and `REJECTED` without it, with
  status `REJECTED`. Liveness acks are answered at once. Blocked steps do not pace. The clock starts when the step committed, so a slow owner turn
  never shortens the next wait. The buffer ends
  with the connection, so it is dropped at disconnect and a resumed connection starts empty.
- **Capability 13 `PACED_MOVEMENT_V1`.** Registered and `offered: true`; it owns no command type or
  domain. `decode_step_result` refuses 4; `decode_step_result_paced(.., true)` admits it.
- **Client.** `apps/client/src/input.rs` reads the same table and `ClickWalk::next_paced_step` waits
  each moved step's duration after its result.
- **Re-measured tests.** The CI seam client paces its first-control and use-wire steps; the expected
  frames are unchanged. Not in scope: creature step timing (CREATURE-MOVE-1), chase steps (RANGED-0).
