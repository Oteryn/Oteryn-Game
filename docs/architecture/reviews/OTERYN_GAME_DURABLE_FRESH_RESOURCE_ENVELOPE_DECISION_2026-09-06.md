## 2. Must decide now

**YES:** B can continue parameterized codec and locking work, but cannot claim complete record/resource acceptance or release its asynchronous boundary with unspecified hard maxima. The smallest decision is one versioned envelope over complete records plus one finite retained-work owner; it does not require a general admission service or deployment design.

The limits constrain accepted record sizes and in-flight concurrency. Raising them later changes memory and overload behavior; lowering persisted-record limits requires an explicit compatibility/migration disposition. Superseding evidence includes representative owner strings, measured peak allocations/SQL result sizes, bounded-load latency, resource-exhaustion findings or a justified larger atomic footprint. Capacity failure may delay admission or recovery; it cannot relax authority, evict a committed obligation or mint a substitute operation.

We do not decide history retention/compaction, database disk/WAL/index quotas, node count, connection-pool topology, production availability, Platform contracts, PKI, provisioning, native source transport or new grace/lease policy. #336 is a separate candidate at this admission and is not an implicit prerequisite. ## 3. First-slice finite table

All sizes are **bytes**, with KiB = 1,024 and MiB = 1,048,576. All maxima are inclusive and independently conjunctive. Registry configurable ranges for this first slice are fixed at the listed maximum; no runtime override can enlarge them. A value meeting one maximum can still fail another or a semantic check. No field is truncated, defaulted or dropped to fit.

| Proposed registry ID | Exact resource / hard maximum | Rationale and allocation impact |
|---|---|---|
| `DFR-OPERATION-BYTES` | Complete canonical encoded fresh operation, standalone publication batch, or claim lifecycle operation/effects: **65,536 bytes each** | One whole atomic operation, including nested provenance and transitions. About 16 times the one measured fresh fixture is headroom, not a throughput estimate; lifecycle maximum is policy pending measurement. No credential/frame mapping is asserted. |
| `DFR-GUARD-BYTES` | One complete canonical encoded typed guard change: **8,192 bytes**, including key, source, precondition, revision and state | Bounds independently stored/reloaded owner observations and each nested guard. Aggregate operation limit still applies. No per-field shortening of owner identity or decision. |
| `DFR-PUBLICATION-CHANGES` | Standalone atomic publication: **4 changes** | Four guard domains suffice for the first admission slice. A larger owner request fails as a whole; it is never silently split. Existing fresh expected set is exactly 4, transition predecessor/successor sets exactly 2 each, by semantics; this row does not replace those exact counts. |
| `DFR-DURABLE-ROW-BYTES` | Sum of logical encoded values in one fresh receipt, guard current/history/high-water, lifecycle receipt, pending checkpoint or affected session/replay/reservation row: **131,072 bytes** | Covers a 64 KiB operation plus duplicated mirrors/keys, original decision time and metadata. Count every variable column, not merely the JSON field. A row may contain at most **32 variable-length SQL columns** (nested fields within a payload are governed by its complete schema/byte bounds). Physical PostgreSQL page/index/WAL overhead is not claimed bounded by this logical row size. |
| `DFR-SQL-RESULT` | Per operation/reconciliation pass: **32 returned SQL payload rows**, **524,288 aggregate logical returned bytes**; bounded nested semantic collections retain their accepted independent counts (mapping below) | Allows one exact operation/session plus four current guards, their bounded history comparisons and bounded attempt aggregates. A returned aggregate remains a payload row; every nested element and byte counts. These are new first-slice policy ceilings, not a claim that every proposed query already fits. Count returned mirrors, status and errors. No unbounded history enumeration or pagination within a pass. |
| `DFR-LOCK-FOOTPRINT` | **64 distinct logical parent/domain lock keys**, **16 relation classes** per operation/pass; exact pending-command children are serialized by their shared attempt/session fence as specified below | Finite room for four guard domains, account/character, replay, predecessor/candidate/session, transport, epoch and existing attempt/nonce keys. Implementation must enumerate its actual footprint and fit; hashing/deduplication does not permit an unbounded input list or weaken typed comparisons. |
| `DFR-QUEUED-WORK` | **8 not-yet-submitted entries**, **524,288 charged resident bytes each**, **4,194,304 bytes total** | A complete sealed request may retain overlapping authorization/transition/operation DTOs; queue accounting includes all copies and metadata. Small burst tolerance is a policy choice. One entry is one operation, not a list of later jobs. |
| `DFR-ACTIVE-WORK` | **2 end-to-end slots**, **4,194,304 charged resident bytes per slot**, **8,388,608 bytes total** | Includes request copies, encoder/decoder storage, SQL arguments/results, locks/keys, checkpoint staging, completion, cancellation and reconcile state. Headroom supports the observed multi-buffer codec and bounded rows without endorsing a measured peak. All stages share the slot. |
| `DFR-COMPLETION` | **1 mailbox entry per active slot**, **131,072 encoded bytes per entry** | Complete operation plus disposition/current snapshot must fit. Its resident representation is charged inside the active 4 MiB budget, not an extra completion queue. |
| `DFR-PENDING-CHECKPOINT` | **2 fixed durable pending slots per logical executor**, **131,072 logical bytes each** | Original complete operation and immutable correlation survive loss/ambiguity. Checkpoints are bounded outstanding-work records, separate from historical receipts. Their in-memory copies remain charged to the active slot. |
| `DFR-WAIT` | Queue residence **1,000 ms**; one DB execution/reconciliation pass **2,000 ms** including lock waits | Sets the requested wait/execution deadline, not proof that a remote backend stopped or that evidence remains valid. Earlier existing authority deadlines still apply. Timeout never proves noncommit and never releases outstanding-work custody. No automatic retry loop. |

The 12 MiB sum of queue and active budgets is a **charged resident-work ceiling per single logical executor**, not a process RSS guarantee. There is one such B executor per process in the first slice; creating a client, port, request, account, task or connection does not create another budget. Fixed code/runtime/database-pool overhead is not a request-sized exemption: any buffer/task/driver allocation retained on behalf of these operations must be charged or have an explicit finite reservation inside these budgets. If a dependency cannot supply a defensible upper bound, executable acceptance remains closed. Actual deployment sizing remains separate.

All variable strings/byte fields are bounded by their containing complete encoded envelope and resident allowance before allocation. The durable-row rule additionally covers mirrors and auxiliary keys not in that envelope. Vector counts are validated before reserve/collection: fixed semantic sets retain exact sizes; standalone batch count is at most four; SQL result rows, lock-key lists and work queues fit their respective explicit maxima. Nested accepted FND02 collections instead retain 64 pending commands and 256 domain revisions per reconnect record; the SQL-row/key limits are not replacement semantic vector limits. Versioned records have finite schema structure; unknown members/versions, arbitrary nesting, attacker-selected collection capacities and compression are not supported. A different codec is allowed only if it proves the same complete-value and allocation bounds without changing semantic/version compatibility.

### Exact inherited FND02 and SQL footprint mapping

Independent review identified a real compatibility ambiguity: at the admission SHA, both `canonical_fnd02_mirrors_are_valid` and `attempt_binding_is_valid` in `admission_journal.rs` fetch individual pending-command rows. Foundation `Fnd02ReconciliationFenceV1` permits **64 pending commands** and **256 domain revisions**. Returning 64 separate SQL rows would exceed the proposed 32-row result limit; reducing either accepted semantic count is not this decision.

For these sibling reads, return **one bounded ordered pending-command aggregate per exact attempt**, in the same protected snapshot as its canonical record/mirrors. Before building the aggregate, validate the complete child count at or below 64 and its complete byte length; reject any overflow rather than truncating, paginating or constructing an unchecked aggregate. Include every command ID/disposition in canonical order. Validate exact count, identity, order and disposition equality against the full canonical pending vector. This is still a payload row, charged with all nested bytes to the 128 KiB row, 512 KiB result and active resident budgets. Returning a summary that ignores child fields is insufficient. SQL aggregation mechanics require qualification; an existing unbounded `fetch_all` does not already satisfy the contract.

Domain revisions remain a nested canonical vector with its own accepted maximum **256**, strict IDs/order and complete full-u64 revisions. They are not 256 SQL rows or lock roots. Decode count before vector allocation and charge all elements, encoded bytes, DTO capacity and copies. Tests must prove a semantically valid reconnect record containing **both all 64 pending commands and all 256 domain revisions** can be encoded, restored and reconciled within the complete byte envelope; resource limits must not be implemented as a smaller hidden semantic cap.

For up to eight attempts in one protected actor epoch, the inherited pending count allows **8 × 64 = 512 pending child rows**, represented by at most eight bounded per-attempt aggregates. The 32 returned-row limit does not cap these physical children at 32. All pending insert/update/delete paths and relevant FK/cascade paths must acquire the same existing attempt/session parent fence before touching children. Lock the exact required child rows before L in the accepted deterministic order, while that parent prevents phantoms and substitutions. The 64-key limit counts the actual shared parent/domain lock roots, not one additional advisory key per command. This mapping is lawful only with proof that every sibling writer participates; a parent lock held by the reader alone is insufficient. Never omit a required attempt, nonce, session or other independently owned key to fit.

The first-epoch footprint has room for four guards, account/character, predecessor/candidate sessions and transports, replay/operation, actor epoch, up to eight attempt roots and their nonce/reservation roots within 64. B must publish its exact deduplicated typed inventory and include its checkpoint key. The observed schemas contain seven 0001 tables (including pending commands) and seven proposed 0002 tables: **14**; one pending-work checkpoint relation would make **15**, leaving one of the 16 relation classes for a genuinely required additional relation. This is an allocation fit, not permission to omit a relation or imply that future source/assignment tables are already present. Any exercised additional relation participates in the same pre-L protocol and budget.

Eight is an **epoch** bound, never a lifetime session/history bound. The inherited V2 predecessor update can cover committed attempts from earlier epochs. B must inventory and protect that exact predicate; it may not narrow the predicate to the current epoch, discard old attempts or page through partial terminalization to satisfy this envelope. The 32-row cap applies to returned client payloads, not to the complete server-side predicate's physical matched rows. B may use server-side locked enumeration/exact validation of that full predicate, under the shared parent/relation serialization and required pre-L physical locks, while returning only a bounded validation/disposition result. Every required comparison and affected row remains covered; a count/hash shortcut that omits required validation is insufficient. This does not require returning every primary key or materializing the entire lifetime attempt set in the client. The requested DB work deadline, charged driver/result budgets and no-detached-work rules still apply. If complete protected coverage cannot finish within the finite pass, return unavailable and roll back all tentative effects; never terminalize a prefix, silently narrow the predicate, free ambiguous custody or mark valid history corrupt because it is large. B must prove the complete-predicate mapping with actual PostgreSQL, including earlier epochs. Historical point replay remains available under its original binding. No retention horizon is chosen.

## 4. Enforcement before allocation and durable effects

1. Acquire a queue reservation before cloning/retaining a sealed caller request. Validate complete encoded sizes using checked length accounting before buffer allocation; add framing, length prefixes, base64 expansion, JSON syntax, repeated strings and all metadata. Check every add/multiply/conversion and **allocated capacity**, not only used length. Size/count preflight must precede semantic validators that clone evidence; validation itself is charged work. Failure is closed. A caller-owned already allocated DTO is not permission for an uncharged adapter copy; producer-side bounding of its own construction remains its owner's obligation.
2. A successful enqueue moves or shares ownership without creating a second uncharged object. Pop into an active slot before allocating SQL state. Any temporary overlap is charged to the destination before transfer, and both budgets remain held until their respective objects are freed. Shared backing storage is counted once only with demonstrable ownership; deep clones count separately. Borrowed references do not justify ignoring the backing allocation.
3. Validate raw encoded length before parsing/base64 allocation. Validate string lengths against remaining bytes and the complete budget before copying; validate count/depth before collection allocation. Decoder canonical re-encoding, error formatting and serialization buffers count. Oversized but otherwise valid values receive a resource failure, not a shortened representation. Malformed historical bytes remain invalid stored state.
4. Before returning stored variable-size values from PostgreSQL, bound the complete row/mirror sizes and cumulative result cardinality. Enforce the length check in the same protected database snapshot as the returned value, before transferring/materializing the oversized payload in the driver; a guarded projection can return a bounded error instead. An unlocked preliminary length query followed by an unrestricted fetch is insufficient. Do not `SELECT *` or fetch all history and then check. Include database parameter copies and row decoding allocations in the active budget.
5. Footprint discovery is bounded before SQL mutation. Acquire all required locks in the accepted deterministic order before L. A newly discovered footprint requires rollback/rebuild under the accepted locking discipline, with no automatic retry inside the current pass. Never omit a lock, row, provenance field or effect to fit. Distinct-key limits are checked before allocating/sorting arbitrarily many keys. All required statement parameters/results and completion capacity are reserved before an irreversible COMMIT can occur.
6. Persist the entire authorized operation, guards, history and lifecycle effect, with exact mirrors. Resource validation failure before commit rolls back all tentative SQL effects, including high-water changes. An oversized stored record is preserved and reported closed as invalid/unavailable stored state; no repair-by-truncation, historical deletion or source metadata fabrication is allowed.

New-work exhaustion maps to a bounded unavailable/capacity outcome at the existing adapter boundary (`Unavailable` where that is the available enum). This decision adds no wire error or Foundation enum. A post-submission timeout/cancellation is **ambiguous** until a definitive durable classification proves otherwise. Logs and errors carry bounded correlation/disposition, never an unbounded copy of rejected input.

## 5. End-to-end ownership, restart and lifecycle

The shared queue/active budgets cover fresh submit, ordinary typed publication, terminal release/replacement, affected claim-preserving lifecycle writes and their direct/reconciled siblings. No alternate reconnect wrapper, detached task or separate publisher can bypass the shared budget for the affected SQL work. Existing unrelated paths retain their own accepted contracts; this does not grant authority to redesign all Durability scheduling.

Before the first possibly effectful database submission, an active slot retains the complete immutable operation and original replay/candidate/transport or publication/lifecycle identity. Establish its fixed pending checkpoint under existing Game executor custody; uncertainty while establishing that checkpoint itself occupies the slot. The checkpoint is work bookkeeping, **not fresh admission PREPARE**, an admission receipt, or live authority. It never consumes the grant or creates claims/session/transport authority independently of accepted final L.

One slot owns submission, every resulting task/driver buffer, at most one completion, ambiguous outcome and one original-operation reconciliation pass at a time. Cancellation, client disconnect, DB timeout, a quickly decoded result, an unread completion or an unavailable database cannot free the slot while any work or unresolved identity remains. Do not detach a task, release a permit and enqueue its callback elsewhere. Stop new work at capacity. A completion is consumed only when the Game owner has accepted its bounded disposition and the adapter has no remaining task, retained result or unresolved checkpoint obligation; this is owner acknowledgement, not a requirement that a disconnected client receive a packet. Receipt classification remains distinct from current controller adoption.

Queue timeout can release a queued operation only after proving it never acquired a DB submission; it cannot reclassify a submitted operation as uncommitted. An ambiguous active operation does not rejoin the queue or receive a new candidate/key. No background freshness chase or automatic DB retry is introduced. An explicitly requested exact retry/reconcile uses the existing slot and identity; each pass obeys the row/byte/time maxima and cannot overlap another pass for that slot.

Restart restores at most the two fixed pending slots before admitting new DB work. Replacement of the executor must fence or terminate predecessor submissions under accepted Game runtime/DB custody and carry pending slots forward; a process restart or fresh registration must not mint empty capacity while predecessor work remains possible. Missing/inconsistent pending custody or an unfenced predecessor keeps activation closed. Reload one bounded original record per slot; recover definitive durable receipt/current snapshot or retain ambiguity. Completion loss reconstructs only that operation's outcome, without minting authority or accumulating mailboxes. Clear/reuse a slot only after definitive disposition and acknowledged delivery/reconciliation, with no pending backend or completion.

Permanent history and tombstones are **not** active-slot entries. Retained receipts, guard history, source/lease floors and transport non-reuse remain durable after a slot is freed. Their individual records and every read are bounded here; cumulative lifetime record count, retention horizon, storage capacity planning and garbage collection are deliberately not selected. No finite resident limit authorizes historical eviction. Terminal release and replacement use the same accounting and preserve exact predecessor/successor evidence. Current source revalidation, original L, rollback, idempotency, post-grace policy and stale-writer fencing remain unchanged.

## 6. Alternatives, costs and compatibility

Choose the explicit finite first-slice envelope plus bounded work custody. Parameterized codecs alone are useful ongoing B work but cannot establish a hard production maximum. Reusing credential/frame or eight-attempt bounds does not cover the same objects. Timeouts without retained slots bound waiting, not submitted SQL or retained results. A large general queue, batch split, history deletion or simplified operation DTO would expand scope or corrupt atomic/replay/provenance semantics.

Two blocked slots can stop progress until original operations are resolved; this is an intentional first-slice availability cost. It is preferable to declaring uncertain work abandoned or allowing uncontrolled growth. The small queue may reject bursts; no fairness or production throughput guarantee is made. Larger deployments or capacity policies require measured evidence and a separately reviewed extension, not additional hidden executors.

This adds missing resource constraints to B's plan only. It does not supersede FND-DUR-FRESH-ADMISSION-V1's no-fresh-PREPARE/final-L/async rules, FND-DUR-FRESH-CLAIM-PUBLICATION-V1's sealed exact effects, FND-04 source provenance/freshness, permanent transport reservations, reconnect attempt bounds or accepted post-grace timing. Historical accepted documents remain unchanged. Neither #336 nor #337 accepts Platform's counterpart or establishes actual source/bootstrap availability.
