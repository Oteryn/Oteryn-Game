## 1. Scope

Item conversions the player triggers, Tibia parity, nothing more. Four target shapes:
- **The used item alone:** a carried item used with no second target becomes another item (UseIntentV1 field 2 or 5 without field 4).
- **A carried item used on a map item or a map object** (UseWithV1 arm 3 `map_item` or arm 4 `map_object`): the carried item converts; the map target is read and is never retired, minted, moved or durably changed by a conversion (C1), except a corpse target of C4b. Digging changes it only in the volatile overlay (C5).
- **A carried item used on another carried item** (the new UseWithV1 arm 6 `carried_item`, §4.1): both are operands (USED and TARGET, C1).
- **An NPC exchange:** an NPC takes items and gives items under a fixed table, no gold (C8), through the NPC trade window (§4.2).

Kinds:
- **Use-with conversions** (crafting combos): a carried item or stack used on a map item, a map object or another carried item becomes one or more items. Reference examples: dough on an oven gives bread (USED dough, count 1, `effect` = TRANSFORM_TO bread with `lifecycle` = REPLACE_INSTANCE; target = the oven's map-object definition, untouched) (`docs/reference/tibia-manual/controls.md`, "Use with", "crafting combos exist"); flour on a vial of water gives a lump of dough and an empty vial (USED flour CONSUME 1, TARGET vial TRANSFORM_TO the same vial at `sub_type` 0, output dough) (Canary `baking.lua`).
- **Fishing:** fishing rod plus worms on a water tile that holds fish. Each successful attempt consumes one worm. The catch chance scales with the Fishing skill. Not every water tile has fish, and a tile does not yield again right after a catch (`docs/reference/tibia-manual/characters.md` §Fishing; `world.md` water rules).
- **Digging:** shovel or pick used on a diggable ground item, which turns it into its opened form (a tile transformation with a timed return), as the upstream tools do (`data/scripts/actions/tools/shovel.lua`, `pick.lua` in the Canary reference).
- **Cooking:** food conversions (wheat to flour at a millstone, dough, baking at an oven) by use-with a heat source or a tool on the map, or on another carried ingredient. The hireling Cook's dishes are a service of HIRE-1 (an NPC-0 BUY); what eating any dish does is C7.
- **NPC exchanges:** an item for an item (Canary Al Dee: a small axe for a pick), several items for one (Canary Uzgod: draconian steel and an obsidian lance for an obsidian knife), item tokens for items (Canary Yana and Cledwyn token shops).

Out of scope, each its own decision: imbuing and the Exaltation Forge (IMBUE-FORGE-0), runes made by spellcasters (MAGIC-ITEMS-1), the Store, the market, coin changing (BANK-0; coin stacks convert 100 to 1 by use, already a bank/item rule), money exchange NPCs (BANK-0), outfit and addon exchanges (outfit decisions), quest dialogue exchanges that move a quest (QUEST-GATE-0 §5.4 keeps them), and new recipes the reference does not have.

## 3. Rules

**C1. The conversion table is content.** One table in `content/` (family `item_conversion`, source-provenance per row), compiled into the bundle. Code holds no recipe; a row absent from the table is not a conversion. A row:
- `input_item`;
- `target`: a map target, named by the definition id of a map item or map object or by a tile kind; `carried` (the target is the row's TARGET operand); or none (a conversion of the used item alone);
- `operands`: one entry for each carried item the conversion touches: `role` = USED, TARGET or INGREDIENT, `item_id`, an optional `sub_type`, `count`, `effect` = CONSUME, TRANSFORM_TO (an item id, for an item that stays in place as another item) or KEEP (a tool such as a rod or shovel), and the effect fields the later rules read, all part of the closed schema: `consume_on` = ALWAYS or SUCCESS (required on an INGREDIENT, rejected elsewhere), and for TRANSFORM_TO `output_item_id`, `lifecycle` = PRESERVE_INSTANCE or REPLACE_INSTANCE (required) and `output_sub_type` (required when the operand matches by `sub_type`, i.e. a fluid container; rejected otherwise). No other operand field exists;
- `outputs` (item id and count list, or a weighted list), `skill` (none or Fishing), `chance_curve` (fishing only, C4);
- `target_effect` (corpse targets only, C4b: TRANSFORM_TO with `output_item_id`, or RETIRE; rejected on every other row);
- `tile_cooldown_ms` (fishing only, integer, required for fishing rows, bounds 1 to 3600000, read by C4; the compile check rejects a fishing row without it and a non-fishing row with it);
- `revert_ms` (digging only, integer, required for digging rows, bounds 1000 to 3600000, read by C5 as the time an opened form stays before it reverts; the compile check rejects a digging row without it and a non-digging row with it);
- `tool` (rod, shovel, pick, none), `capacity_check`, `result_message`.

**The map target is not an operand.** It is matched by its definition (and, for fishing, by the fish presence of C4) and is never retired, minted, moved or durably changed by a conversion; only C5 changes it, in the volatile overlay, and C4b changes a corpse. The compile check rejects a row whose effect would retire, durably transform or mint a map target other than through C4b.

**TARGET is the second carried item.** A row with `target` = `carried` has exactly one TARGET operand; a row with any other `target` has none (compile check). The client names the TARGET instance with UseWithV1 arm 6 (§4.1); the server never chooses it. At PREPARE the handle must resolve to one live entry of the actor's own trees (the main backpack and the open container views of the actor's trees, BAGS-0; equipment slots after ITEM-MOVE-2a), exactly as UseIntentV1 field 2 does, and must not be the USED instance. It then matches by `item_id` plus `sub_type` like any operand, takes any of the three effects (CONSUME, TRANSFORM_TO, KEEP), and follows the partial-stack, split and container rules of C3 like USED. A target of the same combination that lies on the map is not a TARGET: a row with `target` = `carried` never matches a map entry (Canary also accepts a vial lying on the ground; Oteryn keeps map targets read-only, a declared difference). Reference rows (Canary `baking.lua`): flour on a vial of water (USED flour CONSUME 1; TARGET vial TRANSFORM_TO the same vial at `sub_type` 0, PRESERVE_INSTANCE; output lump of dough), flour on a vial of milk (output lump of cake dough), cake dough on a bar of chocolate (both CONSUME 1; output lump of chocolate dough), holy water dough on a bulb of garlic (both CONSUME 1; output lump of garlic dough), garlic dough on a baking tray (USED CONSUME 1; TARGET tray TRANSFORM_TO the tray with garlic cookie dough, REPLACE_INSTANCE), sugar cane on wheat (USED TRANSFORM_TO sugar oat; TARGET CONSUME).

**INGREDIENT is a consumed operand that is neither the used item nor the target:** it names an item the actor holds separately (the fishing worm), has `effect` = CONSUME only, and is resolved by `item_id` (plus `sub_type` when stated) and `count` from the actor's own containers in the server's fixed container order, never chosen by the client, and never the USED or TARGET instance; it carries `consume_on` = ALWAYS or SUCCESS (a fishing worm is SUCCESS: retired in the C3 commit of a successful catch only, never on a failed roll or a refusal). An INGREDIENT the actor lacks in the required count is a refusal before any roll.

**Every TRANSFORM_TO operand declares `lifecycle` = PRESERVE_INSTANCE or REPLACE_INSTANCE (DUR-03 §16), validated per row and never derived from the output:** PRESERVE_INSTANCE keeps the ItemInstance uid, lineage and container contents and changes only its definition or subtype (used for a fluid container emptied or filled); REPLACE_INSTANCE retires the old instance and mints a new one with a new uid in the same C3 transaction (dough to bread), each with the conversion cause for audit. The compile check rejects a TRANSFORM_TO operand with no `lifecycle`, a REPLACE_INSTANCE whose count differs from its operand, and a PRESERVE_INSTANCE that would change a stack count.

**Fluid operands match by `item_id` plus `sub_type`.** Portable fluid contents are ItemInstance subtype state, not an item id (`OTERYN_ITEM_AUTHORING_FORMAL_SCHEMA_V1.md`: current count, subtype, charges and container contents belong to ItemInstance; portable fluid containers stay Item capabilities), so a fluid operand names the container `item_id` and the required fluid `sub_type` (the fluid type enum, e.g. water = 1, milk = 9 in `baking.lua`). An operand with no `sub_type` matches only an instance with subtype 0 (an empty container, or an item with no subtype); it never matches by `item_id` alone across fluid states. The effect on a fluid container is TRANSFORM_TO the same `item_id` at a stated output `sub_type` (an emptied container is `sub_type` 0), or to another container id where the reference does so (holy water: the flask becomes an empty vial), committed as an ItemInstance change in the C3 transaction; the container is never retired or minted unless the row says CONSUME. The compile check rejects a row whose fluid operand lacks `sub_type`, and a parity test covers wrong-fluid and empty-container refusals.

**C2. Deterministic RNG.** The only random conversions are a weighted output and the fishing catch roll. They draw from a new registered RNG purpose `ConversionRngPurpose` (variants `FishingCatch`, `WeightedOutput`), seeded from the world seed, the character id, the item uid and the per-character conversion counter, following the ATTACK-0 §4 RNG-purpose pattern. A fishing row with a weighted output draws `FishingCatch` first and `WeightedOutput` only on success, both from the same counter value. Same inputs, same result. No wall-clock or global RNG. A replayed or retried intent returns the original result (idempotent on the intent id), so a retry cannot reroll. The conversion counter is defined in C2a.

**C2a. The conversion counter is durable and reserved per attempt.** The per-character `conversion_seq` is the number of conversion attempts the character has durably committed. Every attempt that passes the preconditions and the capacity check of C3 and then needs a draw (a fishing roll or a weighted output) is one attempt, and it commits exactly one `ConversionAttempt` record keyed by (character id, intent id) that advances `conversion_seq` by exactly 1, whether the roll succeeds or fails:
- The draw uses the value n = `conversion_seq` read under the `character_root` row lock inside the transaction that will commit, so the seed (world seed, character id, item uid, n) is bound to the durable counter and the intent, never to memory.
- A successful attempt commits the counter advance in the same C3 transaction as the retire and mint. A failed fishing roll commits a counter-only transaction (the `ConversionAttempt` record and its receipt); it retires and mints nothing and consumes no worm. A refusal before the roll (a precondition or C3 capacity refusal) is not an attempt and does not advance the counter.
- Because n advances on every committed attempt, the next fresh attempt with the same rod and tile draws from n+1 and is not a repeat of the failure. The `ConversionAttempt` record keyed by intent id makes a replayed or retried intent return the recorded outcome without a new draw. A crash before commit leaves n unchanged, so the retry redraws the same n and gets the same result, which is correct because nothing was committed; a crash after commit leaves n+1 and the record, so failover and restart cannot change an outcome.
- Classification under DUR-02: `conversion_seq` is a mutable per-character gameplay counter that determines future outcomes, so it is Character-scoped durable state and the item-only exemption of the CHARACTER-REVISION-ITEM-TRANSACTION-COMPOSITION rule (transactions whose only Character effects are item locations and DUR-03 cause records) does not cover it. Every committed `ConversionAttempt` therefore advances `CharacterRevision` exactly once in its own transaction, whether the roll succeeds or fails, and the transaction inserts exactly one registered receipt row for that advance: a new receipt kind, table `game_character_conversion_receipts`, keyed `(character_id, conversion_occurrence_id)` (the intent id), with `request_binding`, `conversion_seq_before` and `conversion_seq_after` (= before + 1) and the shared chain columns `original_character_revision`, `committed_character_revision` (= original + 1), `level_before/after` and `experience_before/after` (unchanged), modelled on `game_character_wheel_receipts` (migration 0070) and added to the `chain` union of the progression guard `game_character_progression_consistency_guard` (the 0073 pattern). A failed-roll attempt that retires and mints nothing still advances the revision and writes the receipt. **The conversion transaction never writes build state:** it does not touch `game_character_build_state` and its receipt carries no skill values, so the 0073 `build_chain` (which accepts skill before/after values only from build and death receipts) is never asked to explain it. A Fishing skill try is not part of this transaction: after the attempt commits, the runtime adds the try to the SKILLS-0 §3.4 live session accumulator, and the build writer commits it with its own build receipt at its normal commits (a level advance, before a death, at logout, or the checkpoint of at most 60 s). A crash may lose at most one checkpoint of tries, as SKILLS-0 §3.4 already accepts. The transaction is fenced on the session generation and serializes on the `character_root` lock. CRAFT-RT-1 adds the migration at the next free number at allocation and confirms this classification in its persistence review.

**C3. Atomic mint and retire.** One durable item conversion is one persistence transaction (volatile MAP-OVERLAY transformations such as digging under C5 are exempt: they retire and mint no items and carry no persistence transaction): the consumed units (of CONSUME operands, of INGREDIENTs whose `consume_on` fires, and of the old instance of a REPLACE_INSTANCE transform) are removed and the outputs (and the new instance of a REPLACE_INSTANCE transform) minted with a conversion cause, while a PRESERVE_INSTANCE transform changes the existing instance in place and retires and mints nothing, or nothing changes. These rules apply to USED and TARGET alike. **Partial stacks keep their remainder (DUR-03 §11.1, §11.5, §12, §13):**
- A CONSUME or INGREDIENT operand of `count` x taken from a stack of quantity q > x decrements that stack in place: it keeps its ItemInstanceId and location with q - x units. Only a stack that reaches 0 retires, in the same transaction. One worm from a stack of 100 leaves the same stack with 99.
- A REPLACE_INSTANCE operand of `count` x on a stack of quantity q > x is a DUR-03 §12 split: the source keeps its ItemInstanceId and location with q - x units of the old definition, and the x transformed units become one fresh ItemInstance of the output definition, placed in the actor's containers like an output and included in the capacity check below. With q = x the old instance retires and the new one takes its location (§16.3).
- A PRESERVE_INSTANCE operand applies only when the selected instance's quantity equals the operand `count` (a non-stackable item, such as a fluid container, or an exact stack); a larger stack is a refusal before any draw (DUR-03 §16.2: one input, one output).
- A CONSUME or REPLACE_INSTANCE operand on a container that holds items is a refusal before any draw; contents are never retired implicitly. The destination of every output is the actor's containers; no conversion places an output on the ground. The capacity check runs before the roll, against the row's worst-case output (the largest weighted output, or the single output). If the outputs do not fit (capacity, container full), the conversion is refused before anything is consumed and before any draw, with the ITEM-LOOP-1 refusal ("not enough room" or "too heavy"): no worm is spent, no counter advance, no skill try. If the state changes between that check and the commit so that the outputs no longer fit, the transaction rolls back whole with the same refusal. No output is dropped to the ground silently.

**C3a. Bounded transaction shape and its DUR-03 admission (DUR-03 §27, §28, §39).** A conversion has a closed, finite participant set; nothing in a row or in the actor's state can widen it.
- **Row ceilings** (CRAFT-CONTENT-1's compile check rejects a row over any of them): at most 4 operands (exactly one USED, at most one TARGET, at most two INGREDIENTs); `outputs` is either a fixed list of 0 to 3 entries or a weighted list of 2 to 24 alternatives, each exactly one entry or the empty alternative (no output; the Canary "rubbish" result of C4b); every operand and output `count` is 1 to 100 and at most the item's `max_stack` (1 for a non-stackable item); only USED and TARGET may be TRANSFORM_TO. Evidence: the reference conversions of §1 and §2 touch at most the used item, one carried target and one INGREDIENT, create at most one item per row except the twelve garlic cookies of one output entry (`baking.lua`), and the largest weighted list is the mechanical rod table of the water elemental remains in C4b (19 alternatives: 3 very rare, 5 rare, 6 common and 5 trash items of `mechanical_fishing.lua`; the dirty water table has 10); the ceilings are ruleset bounds with headroom (DUR-03 §28). A reference row over them needs an amendment of this line, never a silent raise.
- **Resolution:** each operand resolves to exactly one live ItemInstance. USED is the selected carried instance (UseIntentV1 field 2, or field 5 resolved by the server); TARGET is the instance named by UseWithV1 arm 6 (C1); an INGREDIENT is the first stack in the server's fixed container order holding at least `count` units (no gathering across stacks; none means a refusal before any draw). Each output entry mints exactly one fresh ItemInstance into a new direct entry of the actor's containers (no merge into an existing stack in v1). A map target is not a participant (C1), except the corpse of C4b, which counts as one touched item.
- **Transaction ceilings** (rows registered at acceptance, below): touched ItemInstances at most 9 (4 operands, 2 REPLACE_INSTANCE new instances for USED and TARGET, 3 outputs; a corpse row of C4b has no TARGET and no INGREDIENT and stays below; `DUR03-RL-01-CRAFT`); location/custody lines at most 9 (`DUR03-RL-02-CRAFT`); value lines 0 (`DUR03-RL-03` stays 0: no gold side); transform inputs/outputs at most 4 (`DUR03-RL-04-CRAFT`: two transforms, each one input and one output: USED and TARGET, or USED and a C4b corpse); container expansion 0 (`DUR03-RL-05` 0: a container with contents is never consumed or replaced, C3); at most 12 participants (the 9 items, the `character_root` row, the `ConversionAttempt` record or the cause record, and the receipt) and 36 work units (`DUR03-RL-06-CRAFT`: 3 work units per participant, the budget of the first revision's 10 participants and 30 work units); one audit event (`DUR03-RL-07-CRAFT-EVENTS` 1; 2 rejected).
- **Audit size ceilings.** The one-item `DUR03-RL-07-PAYLOAD-BYTES` and `-ENVELOPE-BYTES` rows cover `OneItemTransactionV1` only, not this aggregate, so the shape has its own: the encoded `CONVERSION` payload is at most 42,138 B (`DUR03-RL-07-CRAFT-PAYLOAD-BYTES`: 9 touched items at 4,682 B each, the per-item share of the first revision's 32,768 B ceiling for 7 items, rounded up) and its ANL-01 envelope at most 42,452 B (`DUR03-RL-07-CRAFT-ENVELOPE-BYTES`: the payload ceiling plus the 314 B envelope overhead of the registered NPC trade rows, 27,408 - 27,094, the same length class). Both are fixed hard maxima (configurable range = the maximum, `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), checked on the encoded length before retaining or decoding, with boundary tests max accepted and max+1 rejected; ANL01-EVENT-PAYLOAD-BYTES (196,608) and ANL01-EVENT-ENVELOPE-BYTES (262,144) still apply on top. CRAFT-RT-1 measures the worst case (D50 method: the maximal nine-item aggregate, every key and revision at 512 B, every technical field at 128 B, maximal integers, a 128-byte build id), records it as a boundary test, and its migration admits a stored conversion envelope only up to the envelope row. A measured worst case above either ceiling stops CRAFT-RT-1 for an amendment of this line; the ceilings are never raised silently.
- **Cause.** One closed `ConversionCause {row, occurrence}` covers every line of the transaction: every decrement, retirement and split remainder as its sink, every transform under its row, and every MINT as its source. The occurrence is the intent id, issued by the runtime and bound 1:1 to the command's CommandRef (the C2 idempotency key; DUR-03 §14: same occurrence never mints twice).
- **Records and revision.** A drawing attempt writes the `ConversionAttempt` record and the C2a receipt (`CharacterRevision` + 1). A certain conversion (no draw) changes no Character state beyond item locations and its DUR-03 cause record, so it falls under the item-only exemption of the composition rule: one DUR-03 cause record keyed by (occurrence, character) under the item writer's fence and the `character_root` lock, and no `CharacterRevision` advance.
- **Audit.** One event per transaction carries one closed `CONVERSION` aggregate: for each touched ItemInstance its id, the mutation class (DECREMENT, RETIRE, SPLIT, TRANSFORM_PRESERVE, TRANSFORM_REPLACE, MINT), lifecycle, location, definition, subtype and quantity before and after, and the cause, plus the conservation summary (DUR-03 §39 minimum evidence). For this shape only it supersedes the §39.1 exclusion of burn and of multiple touched items and the §39.3 statement that a MINT first establishes Ground custody (outputs are minted straight into container entries). Every other §39 obligation (fences, idempotency, current authority, conservation) is unchanged.
- **Admission gate.** The shape is admitted when this decision is accepted: the acceptance PR adds the CRAFT-1 amendment pointer to DUR-03 §39.3 and the CRAFT rows named above (`DUR03-RL-01-CRAFT` to `DUR03-RL-06-CRAFT` and `DUR03-RL-07-CRAFT-EVENTS`, `-PAYLOAD-BYTES`, `-ENVELOPE-BYTES`), and the exchange rows of C8, to `docs/contracts/RESOURCE_LIMITS_REGISTRY.json`. CRAFT-RT-1 and CRAFT-NPC-1 are not allocated before both are on main.

**C4. Fishing.**
- Preconditions: the fishing row is rod = USED (`effect` KEEP), the water tile = the map target (a tile kind, C1), and the bait = an INGREDIENT operand (`effect` CONSUME, `consume_on` SUCCESS, count 1) resolved from the actor's containers per C1; a target water tile with fish. No bait means a refusal and no roll; the bait is retired in the same C3 transaction as the catch mint, on success only. The attempt then follows C3 (capacity before the roll) and C2a (counter).
- **Source multiplicity (`GAME-CHANNEL-01`): `ChannelLocalRepeatable`, eligibility scope `CharacterWorld`.** Fishing is a repeatable source, not a unique or shared-eligibility one. The simulation is channel-local: the fish presence and the tile cooldown are keyed by (`ChannelId`, tile), so the same water tile in two channels has two independent cooldowns and a catch in one channel does not cool the other. No durable eligibility is shared across channels, because the durable value is bounded without it: every successful catch consumes one bait (a retirement), the catch roll is skill-gated, and the per-character `conversion_seq` and the C3 transaction apply in every channel alike, so a character gains no extra attempt by changing channel beyond what its own bait supply and actions allow. The World stays the one economy boundary (bait and fish are World items); the owner can later re-class fishing as `ChannelLocalSharedEligibility` by amending this line, which changes only the cooldown key to (`WorldId`, tile).
- **The catch curve** (Canary `fishing.lua` and `mechanical_fishing.lua`, both the same): chance in percent = min(max(10 + (S - 10) x 0.597, 10), 50), S the effective Fishing skill (SKILLS-0), success when a uniform U in 1 to 100 is at most the chance. The row's `chance_curve` stores it in integers, exactly equivalent: success when 1,000 x U <= clamp(10,000 + 597 x (S - 10), 10,000, 50,000). The skill-try rule of SKILLS-0 applies (one Fishing try per committed attempt, success or failure, as the reference adds one per attempt); this brief only names the hook, SKILLS-0 owns the amount. The hook feeds the SKILLS-0 §3.4 live accumulator after the attempt commits and never writes build state in the conversion transaction (C2a); the accumulator is delivered by W2b. A refusal (no bait, no fish, no room) is not an attempt and gives no try; the mechanical rod script adds a try even without bait, a declared difference (`PARITY_PENDING` in the row provenance).
- **Tile reservation (DUR-03 §7).** The attempt reserves the (`ChannelId`, tile) fish availability in PREPARE, before the draw and before yielding to persistence, in the same volatile channel-local state as the cooldown. A second attempt on a reserved tile is refused before any draw with the fixed "no fish" message (no counter advance, no bait, no skill try). A committed successful catch turns the reservation into the cooldown; a committed failed roll, a refusal or a rollback releases it; an ambiguous persistence outcome keeps it until reconciliation decides commit (cooldown) or no commit (release). So only one successful catch can consume a tile's availability.
- A tile that just yielded does not yield again for the row's `tile_cooldown_ms`, measured on the deadline clock of the world (not wall time). The cooldown is channel-local volatile simulation state keyed by (`ChannelId`, tile), not persisted across a crash (like the ADR-0021 volatile map state). It is set only after a committed successful catch; a failed roll does not start it.
- **Destination policy: one outcome.** A catch is minted only into the player's container; there is no ground destination. When the container has no room for the worst-case catch, the attempt is refused under C3 before the roll: no roll, no bait consumed, nothing created, no counter advance, no skill try, and the tile cooldown is not started.

**C5. Digging and tile transformation.** A carried shovel or pick used on a diggable ground item (UseWithV1 arm 3 or 4; ingress, reach and security gates are WORLDINT-USE-1's) replaces it with the opened form for the row's `revert_ms` (C1) and returns it afterwards. The change is volatile map state in the per-channel MAP-OVERLAY-1a layer (hidden and added entries) under ADR-0021 (a crash resets it, as does the planned world reset). Items stacked on the tile move with it per the ITEM-LOOP-1 tile rules. No permanent tile edit is allowed. Where WORLD_INTERACTION-0 §6 already defines the target as a LocalObject (the loose stone pile and secret holes of FLOOR-1), that transition is FLOOR-1's and this decision does not redefine it; CRAFT-DIG-1 covers only the other diggable ground items of the reference, each with `revert_ms` = the opened form's items.xml `duration` (for example the hole 594: 60 s, returning to 593). Digging yields stay out (WORLD_INTERACTION-0 defers them to their own decision).

**C6. Refusals are fixed messages** from the table's `result_message` set; no new free text in code.

**C7. Cooking, timed items and dish effects (TIMED-ITEM-0B hook).**
- **Outputs.** A conversion output (or a REPLACE_INSTANCE new instance) whose definition is an admitted timed definition (TIMED-ITEM-0B) is minted with no `game_item_timed_states` row: the absent row means the definition's full values at revision 0 (TIMED-ITEM-0B §4, lazy rows). The conversion writes no timed line and no `TimedItemCause`; its first timed write is the owner of that item's first use, equip or expiry (TIMED-ITEM-0B §5 to §8).
- **Operands.** A timed definition may be an operand only with `effect` KEEP (a frozen tool read in place; a live item is in an equipment slot and never resolves as an operand, C1). The compile check rejects a timed definition as a CONSUME, INGREDIENT or TRANSFORM_TO operand: retiring or transforming a timed instance would need a timed line that TIMED-ITEM-0B §12 does not admit for this shape. No reference conversion consumes a timed item. TIMED-ITEM-0B rejects stackable timed definitions (§4, "No stacking"), so a timed output is always one non-stackable instance.
- **Dish effects.** What eating a dish does is the dish's item-use semantics on the ITEM-USE-0 path, not a conversion. Plain food (bread, cakes, cookies, fish) gives `FOOD_REGENERATION` time (ITEM-USE-0, FOOD-REGEN-1, content `regeneration_seconds` of ITEM-SEM-USE). The hireling Cook's gourmet dishes (HIRE-1 H5) give one condition each, from `hireling_foods.lua`: shielding +7, distance fighting +7, magic level +3, melee skills +3, speed +80, fishing +30, each for 3,600,000 ms; or an instant heal of 30 % of maximum hit points, or of 30 % of maximum mana; one dish has no effect. All gourmet dishes share one cooldown of 600 s ("special-foods-cooldown"); a use inside it is refused with the `EXHAUSTED` disposition. Each condition is a CONDITIONS-0 instance with source kind `item` and the dish definition as provenance (CONDITIONS-0 §3.2 as amended by TIMED-ITEM-0B §11.2); its remaining time is durable as COND-DUR-1 makes condition time durable. CRAFT-FOOD-1 delivers these semantics.

**C8. NPC exchanges.** An NPC takes items and gives items under a fixed table, never gold. The exchange is a third side of the NPC-0 trade writer (NPC-TRADE-1), not a new writer.
- **Content: family `npc_exchange`** (CRAFT-NPC-1), compiled into the bundle with source provenance per row. A row: `npc` (an NPC of the NPC-0 catalogue), `exchange_key` (stable, unique per NPC), `take` (1 to 3 lines) and `give` (1 to 3 lines), each line `item_id`, optional `sub_type` (fluid and charged items, the NPC-0 §5.4 matching rule) and `count` (1 to 100, at most the item's `max_stack`), an optional `gate` (a read-only QUEST-GATE-0 §5.1 quest condition: at most `QUESTGATE0-RL-01` predicates over the session's quest state, plus level and `holds_item`), and `result_message` lines. Content validation refuses a coin item in `take` or `give` (no gold, as QUEST-GATE-0 §5.4 refuses coin keys in declared items), a line over the bounds, a duplicate `exchange_key`, and more than `CRAFT1-RL-01` rows for one NPC. An item token (Canary gold token 22721, silver token 22516) is an item, not gold, and is allowed. A row whose `gate` the bundle cannot evaluate yet is held, as NPC-0 holds `LUA_PREDICATE` nodes (D9), never served ungated. Evidence for the bounds: Canary Al Dee (1 take line, 1 give line), Uzgod (2 take lines), Yana (up to 3 give lines, counts up to 25), Cledwyn (token prices up to 100, 23 offers).
- **Scope against quests.** An exchange that must also move a quest stays the QUEST-GATE-0 §5.4 dialogue exchange (NPC-QUEST-1). C8 exchanges never write quest state; their `gate` only reads it.
- **Mapping onto C3a.** An exchange row is a conversion with no USED item, no TARGET, no draw and no transform: each `take` line is a CONSUME operand resolved like an NPC-0 SELL (one live direct entry of the main backpack holding at least `count` units of the item, first in B3 display order, a stack in part or whole, no contents and the default state apart from quantity and the matched `sub_type`; no gathering across stacks; DUR-03 §11.1, §11.5), and each `give` line is a fixed output minted as one fresh stack into a new direct entry of the main backpack, as NPC-0 BUY mints (free entries counted after the takes; a whole take frees its entry). Too few free entries, a missing take, a failed `gate` or a stale catalogue revision rejects the whole transaction before any write.
- **Transaction.** One item-only transaction under the NPC-0 §5.1 and §5.2 rules (open conversation, talk range, live session, the item writer's fence and lock order, one occurrence per command bound to its CommandRef, a replay returning the first outcome): no `CharacterRevision` advance and no receipt (composition §3 rule 1), one DUR-03 cause record keyed by (occurrence, character) binding the NPC, the exchange (catalogue revision and index), the side `EXCHANGE` and every line. The `gate` is evaluated again under `character_root` inside the transaction, as QUEST-GATE-0 §5.1 revalidates. The cause is `NpcTradeCause {npc, offer, side: EXCHANGE, occurrence}`: the take BURNs use it as their burn sink and the give MINTs as their MINT source (the SELL and BUY patterns of NPC-0 §5.5); it is never a `FeeBurnCause`. CRAFT-NPC-1 widens the `0023` entry-removal proof and the NPC trade cause record's side check to `EXCHANGE` at the next free migration number.
- **DUR-03 rows** (registered at acceptance, owner contract this decision, `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, max and max+1 tests): `DUR03-RL-01-NPC-TRADE-EXCHANGE` 6 ItemInstances (3 takes and 3 gives), `DUR03-RL-02-NPC-TRADE-EXCHANGE` 6 location lines, `DUR03-RL-06-NPC-TRADE-EXCHANGE-PARTICIPANTS` 6 and `DUR03-RL-06-NPC-TRADE-EXCHANGE-EFFECT-WORK-UNITS` 15 (3 whole burns x 3 + 3 MINTs x 2, the NPC trade work-unit method). Value lines 0. The audit event is the NPC trade event: `DUR03-RL-07-NPC-TRADE-EVENTS` 1 and the existing `DUR03-RL-07-NPC-TRADE-PAYLOAD-BYTES` (27,094) and `-ENVELOPE-BYTES` (27,408) rows, which bound a BUY of 23 items and so cover the 6 of an exchange; CRAFT-NPC-1 adds a boundary test of the maximal exchange under them.

## 4. Wire

A conversion of the used item alone is UseIntentV1 field 2 (or 5) without field 4, with the ITEM-USE-1c ingress. A carried item used on a map item or map object (crafting onto a map entry, cooking, fishing, digging, a corpse of C4b) is UseIntentV1 field 4, UseWithV1 arm 3 `map_item` or arm 4 `map_object` under capabilities 15 and 11, with the WORLDINT-USE-1 ingress (WORLD_INTERACTION-0 §3, which owns the reach and security gates). The entity arm and the rune-only position arm carry no conversion. Two additions, each under protocol review and the `PROTOCOL_OTERYN_V1_REGISTRY` rules (IDs stable and never reused, capabilities additive and optional, unknown enum values fail closed, no Canary ID imported); capability numbers are reserved on the protocol registry issue #162 by the wire child at allocation, never in this brief.

**4.1 Carried target (CRAFT-WIRE-1).**
- `UseWithV1` gains arm 6, `oteryn.protocol.v1.item_view.ItemTargetV1 carried_item = 6`: a non-zero handle of an entry in the actor's own trees, as UseIntentV1 field 2 names the used item (ITEM-VIEW handles, BAGS-0). Exactly one arm, as today; valid only with UseIntentV1 field 2 or 5, at most once.
- A new optional capability `ITEM_USE_CARRIED_TARGET_V1` requires capabilities 15 `ITEM_USE_V1` and 4 `ITEM_VIEW_MOVE_V1`. A session that did not select it and sends arm 6 fails closed (`REJECTED`), as arm 5 does without capability 22.
- Dispositions, all existing values (no new enum value), owned by this capability: `ITEM_USE_CARRIED_TARGET_V1` adds `TARGET_ILLEGAL` (today emitted only under capability 22 `RUNE_USE_V1`) and `NOT_POSSIBLE` and `NO_ROOM` (today only under capability 11) to the values an arm-6 intent may receive, independent of capabilities 11 and 22. A client that selects the capability declares it decodes these values; capability negotiation (`PROTOCOL_OTERYN_V1_REGISTRY`) records them in the capability's disposition set, and CRAFT-WIRE-1 tests an arm-6 refusal of each value in a session that selected neither 11 nor 22. A session without the capability cannot send arm 6, so never receives them from it. Mapping: a zero handle, a second arm or arm 6 with UseIntentV1 field 1 is `REJECTED`; a handle that resolves to no live entry of the actor's own trees is `NO_TARGET`; a handle of an older view generation is `STALE_STATE`; a handle that names the USED instance is `TARGET_ILLEGAL`; no conversion row for the (used, target) pair is `NOT_POSSIBLE`; the C3 capacity refusal is `NO_ROOM` (or `FULL`, as ITEM-USE-0 maps a full container).
- Bounds: arm 6 is at most 13 bytes (tag, length and an ItemTargetV1 of at most 11 bytes), smaller than the `map_object` arm, so the intent stays within `ITEMUSE0-RL-03` (529 B) and no byte row changes; CRAFT-WIRE-1 adds a boundary test of the largest arm-6 intent under that row.
- Ingress: CRAFT-WIRE-1 decodes arm 6, resolves the handle at PREPARE (C1, TARGET) and hands the pair to the CRAFT-RT-1 engine; reach is the actor's own trees, so no distance gate applies.

**4.2 NPC exchange (CRAFT-NPC-WIRE-1).**
- A new optional capability `NPC_EXCHANGE_V1` requires capability 3 `NPC_SERVICE_V1`. `NpcTradeSide` gains `NPC_TRADE_SIDE_EXCHANGE = 3`.
- `NpcTradeWindowV1` (domain 8) gains field 4, `repeated NpcExchangeOfferV1 exchanges = 4`, at the same `catalogue_revision` as the offers; `NpcExchangeOfferV1 {repeated NpcExchangeLineV1 take = 1; repeated NpcExchangeLineV1 give = 2;}` and `NpcExchangeLineV1 {uint32 item_definition_ref = 1; uint32 count = 2; uint32 sub_type = 3;}`. The server sends field 4 only to a session with the capability; a session without it never sees an exchange, and field 3 never carries an EXCHANGE offer.
- Intent: the existing `NpcTradeIntentV1` (command 8) with `side` = EXCHANGE, `offer_index` indexing field 4, `quantity` = 1 and `expected_unit_price` = 0; any other quantity or price, or the side without the capability, is `REJECTED`; a catalogue revision mismatch is `STALE` and re-projects the window (NPC-0 §5.1). The intent's size does not change.
- Rows (registered by CRAFT-NPC-WIRE-1 before implementation, client visible, `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, max and max+1 tests): `CRAFT1-RL-01` exchange offers per NPC window, 64 (reference maximum 23, Cledwyn); `CRAFT1-RL-02` lines per side of one exchange offer, 3; `CRAFT1-RL-03` one encoded `NpcTradeWindowV1` with field 4 under the capability, 30,378 B: the `NPC0-RL-03-BYTES` window of 22,570 B plus 64 exchange elements of at most 122 B (tag, length, and six lines of at most 20 B: three varint fields of at most 6 B each plus tag and length). `NPC0-RL-03-BYTES` stays the bound for a session without the capability. Both are within the FND-02 snapshot chunk bound (`FND02-SNAPSHOT-CHUNK-BYTES` 524,288).
