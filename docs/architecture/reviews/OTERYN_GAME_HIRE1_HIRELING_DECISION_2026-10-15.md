## 1. What a hireling is (reference)

From `docs/reference/tibia-manual/products.md` §Hirelings and `world.md`:
- NPCs placed in houses; maximum 10 per character. Gender and name are chosen at purchase; names follow the Tibia Rules and a violation gets a random replacement.
- The base **Hireling Apprentice** greets guests and sells basic items.
- **Apprenticeships** unlock services: **Banker** (bank and guild bank access), **Trader** (city trade goods), **Steward** (stash access), **Cook** (food preparation, fee-based). All hirelings of one character share the unlocked services.
- **Hireling dresses** are cosmetic outfits; ownership limits outfit allocation (two hirelings need two copies of a dress).
- **Hireling services:** name change and sex change, visible only to a player who owns hirelings.
- A hireling is sold through the Store as house furniture ("NPCs can be purchased via the Store as hireling-type furniture for houses").

Upstream behaviour (Canary reference; local clone of `opentibiabr/canary`, the pinned import revision of `imports/canary/sources.json` for the item data):
- `data/libs/systems/hireling.lua`: a hireling is a durable per-character record (`player_hirelings`: owner, name, sex, active flag, position, outfit), not an item. Unlocked services (`HIRELING_SKILLS`: Banker, Cooking, Steward, Trader) and owned dresses (`HIRELING_OUTFITS`: banker, cooker, steward, trader, servant, hydra, ferumbras, bonelord, dragon) are per-character flags shared by all the character's hirelings. Sex is male or female; the default outfit is the Citizen look (1107 female, 1108 male) and each dress has a male and a female look. At startup a hireling whose house is no longer owned by its owner leaves the house (`checkHouseAccess`). A player talks to a hireling only inside the same house (`canTalkTo`), and only the owner may send it away (`returnToLamp`: "You are not the master of this hireling.").
- `data/scripts/actions/items/hireling_lamp.lua`: the hireling appears on the owner's own position; it is refused outside a house, on a house door tile, on a tile that already holds a hireling, and in a house the user does not own. **Oteryn keeps these placement rules and drops the lamp** (owner direction above): the lamp is only Canary's container for an unplaced record.
- `data/libs/gamestore/purchases.lua` and `data/modules/scripts/gamestore/catalog/house_hirelings.lua`: the Store refuses a hireling purchase at the per-character maximum (`GameStore.ItemLimit.HIRELING`; the offer text says "maximum amount that can be owned by character: 10"), validates the name, and sells the Banker, Cook, Steward and Trader skills, the name change and the sex change as separate offers. Store prices there are Tibia Coin amounts; Oteryn prices are the Platform's decision, not this brief's.
- `data-otservbr-global/npc/hireling.lua`: the owner-only topic to send the hireling away or change its outfit; the bank, stash and food topics; the Cook's dish fees (random dish 15,000 gold, chosen dish 90,000 gold), which are content values for HIRE-JOBS-1.

## 2. Rules

**H1. A hireling is a Store product, delivered as a record.** The Game never sells one for gold or in an NPC dialogue, and no item represents a hireling anywhere (no lamp, no token, no inbox entry). The Game receives a **Store delivery** for a character: `HIRELING` (a new hireling, payload: name and sex), `HIRELING_SKILL` (an apprenticeship), `HIRELING_NAME_CHANGE`, `HIRELING_SEX_CHANGE` or `HIRELING_DRESS` (payload: one of the dresses of H7). Each delivery is an idempotent grant keyed by the Platform's delivery id, consumed once, exactly as the Premium entitlement consumer (PREM-1) consumes entitlements. A `HIRELING` delivery creates the hireling row directly in state UNPLACED (H2). `HIRELING_NAME_CHANGE`, `HIRELING_SEX_CHANGE` and `HIRELING_DRESS` deliveries only grant a counted per-character entitlement (H6, H7); they never change a hireling. Applying one to a hireling is a separate player command (§3, session fence).

**H2. Records.** A hireling is a private NPC: a durable row owned by the character, not by a house and not by the NPC content catalogue. Columns: `hireling_id` (UUID, the durable key), `owner_character_id`, `name`, `sex` (MALE or FEMALE), `outfit` (the Citizen default of H7 or an allocated dress), `state` (UNPLACED or PLACED), and, only when PLACED, `world_id`, `house_key` and `position` (a tile of `game_house_tiles`, HOUSE-OWN-0 §3), plus a monotonic `revision`. The apprenticeship set is per **character** (shared by all its hirelings, as the manual and Canary say), a bitset of the four services. A `HIRELING_SKILL` delivery for a service the character has already unlocked is refused with a defined code through the delivery status callback (§4) before any change, never applied as a no-op. At most `HIRE1-RL-01` (10) hirelings per character, counted over every state: a `HIRELING` delivery that would make the 11th is refused with the defined code `HIRELING_LIMIT` through the status callback before any change, never silently dropped (Canary refuses the purchase at the same bound). A hireling row is never deleted while its owner's root exists (HOUSE-OWN-0 §3 owner lifecycle: roots are never deleted); a future character deletion workflow decides its end.

**H3. Placement and removal (no item).**
- **Place.** The owner sends the HIRE-WIRE-1 place command naming one of their UNPLACED hirelings. The hireling is placed on the **actor's own tile** (the Canary position rule), in one transaction that sets the row PLACED with that house and position. Under the locks of §3, before any change, the transaction requires, else refuses with a typed code and writes nothing: the actor's session is in the house scope (HOUSE-RUNTIME-0) of a house whose property is `OWNED` or `MOVE_OUT_PENDING` with the actor as owner (`NOT_HOUSE_OWNER`); the tile is a house tile of that house (`NOT_HOUSE_TILE`); the tile is not a door of the house in the House catalogue (a door is its house key and position, HOUSE-OWN-0 §2) (`DOOR_TILE`); no PLACED hireling stands on that tile (`TILE_TAKEN`, `HIRE1-RL-02`: one hireling per tile); the named hireling is the actor's and UNPLACED (`NOT_FOUND` or `ALREADY_PLACED`). A guest, a subowner or any other non-owner is refused. Several hirelings of one owner may stand in one house, each on its own tile; the per-character maximum bounds them.
- **Remove.** Only the owner removes a hireling: either by the hireling's dialogue (the owner-only topic of the Canary NPC, confirmed by `yes` within `NPC0-RL-06`, the NPC-0 §4 confirmation pattern) or by the HIRE-WIRE-1 remove command while the owner stands in the same house scope. Both reach one writer: under the locks of §3 and before any change, the acting character must be the hireling's `owner_character_id`, its session must be in the house scope that holds the hireling, and the row must be PLACED; any other actor (a guest included) is refused with a typed code and nothing changes. The transaction sets the row UNPLACED and clears the house and position. The record, its name, sex, outfit and services stay; it can be placed again later, in the same or another house the owner then owns.
- **The row is the durable truth**, never the NPC entity. The NPC is an entity of the house scope (HOUSE-RUNTIME-0: one runtime scope per active house, keyed by the HouseId) spawned from every PLACED row of that house when the scope activates, after a placement commits, and removed after a removal or a return (H4) commits. Its runtime entity identity is issued by the hosting scope and never persisted. It is seen by the sessions in that house scope, does not move, has no combat or pathing and is not attackable (an NPC, NPC-0 rules). Talking to it follows NPC-0 (talk range `NPC0-RL-07`, open conversations `NPC0-RL-05`) and, as in Canary, only a character in the same house scope can open a conversation with it.

**H4. House loss and house transfer.** A PLACED hireling exists only while its house is owned by its owner. The invariant is held by the database: a deferred guard (HIRE-DATA-1) refuses any commit that leaves a PLACED row whose property is not `OWNED` or `MOVE_OUT_PENDING` by `owner_character_id`, whose position is not a tile of that house, or whose position is a door of the house. The writers that change these facts unplace the hirelings themselves, in their own transaction:
- **Disposition** (move-out, eviction, catalogue retirement; HOUSE-OWN-0 §7): the fence transaction of step 1 sets every PLACED hireling of the house UNPLACED (the BED-0 §6 pattern for sleeper rows), before any interior item moves, with return cause `disposition`.
- **Any other owner change** of the property (a future direct transfer between players, or any other transition that changes the owner without a disposition): the transaction that changes the owner sets every PLACED hireling of the previous owner in that house UNPLACED, with return cause `owner_change`. HOUSE-OWN-0 does not define a direct transfer yet (§15, "deliberately not decided"); whichever decision adds one inherits this line.
- **Catalogue refresh** that removes a tile from the house or makes it a door: the refresh transaction of `game_house_tiles` (HOUSE-OWN-0 §3) sets every PLACED hireling on an affected position UNPLACED, with return cause `catalogue`.
Nothing is lost: the record, its outfit and its services stay with the owner, who can place it again in a house they own. When the house scope activates, a PLACED row that fails the invariant is a defect: the scope does not spawn it and reports it (it cannot occur after a committed guard). A hireling never wanders and never leaves its tile on its own.

**H5. Job set.** The hireling NPC offers, by dialogue and trade, only what its owner has unlocked. It serves its owner and the characters the house rules admit to the house scope (HOUSE-ACL-1 entry rights; HOUSE-RUNTIME-0 §3: only invited characters enter):
- **Apprentice (always):** greets guests (a voice line) and sells basic items from a fixed catalogue (NPC-0 trade, content).
- **Banker:** the owner's bank (BANK-0 balance, deposit, withdraw, transfer) and the guild bank where the owner has rank; fees as in BANK-FEE-0. Guests get no bank access.
- **Trader:** the city trade goods catalogue (equipment, tools, potions, runes, wands, rods), the same NPC-0 trade writer with a hireling catalogue.
- **Steward:** access to the owner's stash (SUPPLY-STASH-0: stow, withdraw, list). The same stash, not a copy; the same limits.
- **Cook:** food preparation for a fee is an NPC-0 BUY (NPC-0 §5.3) of a dish from the hireling Cook catalogue, through the same NPC-0 trade writer as the Trader; it adds no writer, fee source or mint cause of its own. It therefore inherits that writer's rules unchanged: the dish mints into the customer's main backpack only and a result that does not fit rejects the whole transaction before anything is written (NPC-0 §5.1, §5.3); the price is paid coins first with only the shortfall debited from the bank (BANK-FEE-0 §3), under the closed NPC BUY fee source (BANK-FEE-0 §5); the command is fenced on the customer's live session and the item writer's fence and lock order (NPC-0 §5.1 and §5.2, composition §3 rules 2-5); and its DUR-03 cause record keyed by the occurrence makes a replay return the first outcome without a second debit or mint (NPC-0 §5.2). The fee debit and the dish mint commit in that one transaction or nothing changes. The recipe list and the fee values are content (Canary: random dish 15,000 gold, chosen dish 90,000 gold); Cook rows live in the hireling catalogue, not in CRAFT-1. What eating a dish does is the dish's item-use semantics (CRAFT-1 C7 and its CRAFT-FOOD-1 child), not a hireling rule.
Guests (other characters in the house scope) may use the Apprentice, the Trader and the Cook (a guest Cook customer pays the fee and receives the food, as above). Owner-only services (Banker, Steward) refuse a guest.

**H6. Name and sex.** A hireling name follows the character-name rules (at most 29 ASCII letters, `CHARACTER_AUTHORITY_PLATFORM_BOUNDARY` §6.1, the bound GUILD-0 also uses for guild names; `HIRE1-RL-03`) and the Tibia Rules of the account decisions; a violation at delivery is replaced by a deterministic placeholder (the manual's random replacement, made reproducible). A name or sex change is two writers: the Store delivery H1 grants one counted name-change or sex-change entitlement to the character (delivery-id fenced, no hireling touched), and the owner applies it to one of their hirelings by a player command that consumes one entitlement and changes the hireling in one session-fenced transaction (§3). Applying a name change is cancellable when the name is unavailable (the manual); a cancelled or refused application keeps the entitlement. Sex selects the male or female look of the current outfit; the outfit choice is preserved otherwise. The application works on an UNPLACED or a PLACED hireling and needs no house.

**H7. Dresses.** The outfit set is content from the Canary reference: the Citizen default (always owned, not counted) and the nine dresses (banker, cook, steward, trader, servant, hydra, ferumbras, bonelord, dragon), each with a male and a female look. A dress is a counted outfit entitlement per character; allocating a dress to a hireling consumes one copy of that dress for as long as the hireling wears it (two hirelings, two copies). A hireling returned to Citizen frees its copy. The allocation is a player command of the owner on one of their hirelings (UNPLACED or PLACED), session-fenced (§3). Unapplied name-change, sex-change and per-dress entitlement counts are bounded by `HIRE1-RL-04` (10 each: a copy or change beyond the hireling maximum can never be used); a delivery over it is refused through the status callback.

**H8. Refusals** are fixed typed codes; no gold is ever taken by the Game in H1 to H7 (the Cook and Trader purchases excepted, H5).

## 3. Persistence and economy

- **No item shape.** A hireling is not an item: no MINT, retirement, location line or DUR-03 cause belongs to H1 to H4, H6 or H7, and no Inbox delivery exists. The only item writes of HIRE-1 are the Apprentice, Trader and Cook purchases of H5, which are NPC-0 trade transactions with NPC-0's own rows (`DUR03-RL-0N-NPC-TRADE-BUY` and `-SELL`).
- Hireling rows, the apprenticeship set and the entitlement counts are character-scoped persistent state. They are not part of the house item custody family. The H4 unplacing runs inside the house writer's transaction (disposition fence, owner change, catalogue refresh) under the HOUSE-OWN-0 lock order; the lock-order line of that decision is amended in HIRE-DATA-1's own packet, not here. Placement and removal take the actor's `character_root` lock, then the house property row (shared, in the HOUSE-OWN-0 order), then the hireling rows of the house in `hireling_id` order; HIRE-DATA-1's persistence review confirms the order against HOUSE-OWN-0 and BED-0.
- **Session fence.** Every player-command write (place and remove of H3, and the application of a name, sex or dress entitlement to a hireling, H6 and H7) is fenced on the acting character's current session generation (AGENTS.md L16): the write carries the generation it was admitted under and is rejected with a typed stale-session refusal when it no longer matches, before any row changes. A stale session can neither place nor remove a hireling. The dialogue removal (H3) is that same command, invoked by the confirmed dialogue occurrence (NPC-0 §4.1, one pending service invocation per conversation). A Cook or Trader purchase (H5) is an NPC-0 trade and carries NPC-0's own fence. Store deliveries (H1), including the grant of a name-change, sex-change or dress entitlement, are not player commands and are fenced by the delivery id instead; no mutation is both.
- **CharacterRevision (DUR-02 rule 2).** Hireling rows, the per-character apprenticeship set, the per-character name-change, sex-change and dress entitlement counts, the name, sex and outfit of a hireling and the dress allocation (H2, H6, H7) are Character-scoped durable state, so every committed transaction that changes any of them is a Character semantic transaction and advances the owner's `CharacterRevision` exactly once for that transaction (a placement, a removal, a house return, a name, sex or dress change, an apprenticeship grant, a delivery that creates a row or grants an entitlement). There is no item in these transactions, so the item-only rule of the CHARACTER-REVISION-ITEM-TRANSACTION-COMPOSITION decision never applies to them.
- **Receipt kind (guard union).** The deferred progression guard (`0073_character_progression_guard_wheel_union.sql`, `game_character_progression_consistency_guard`) requires every `CharacterRevision` advance to be represented by exactly one registered receipt in its `chain` union (count and distinct `committed_character_revision` equal to root revision minus 1). HIRE-1 therefore adds a new registered receipt kind, table `game_character_hireling_receipts`, keyed `(character_id, hireling_occurrence_id)` (a UUID minted per transaction and bound to the intent id, to the delivery id for a Store delivery, or to the house operation for a return), with `kind` in (`PLACEMENT`, `REMOVAL`, `DELIVERY`, `APPRENTICESHIP`, `NAME`, `SEX`, `DRESS`, `HOUSE_RETURN`), `hireling_id` (NULL for an apprenticeship or an entitlement grant), `request_binding`, for `HOUSE_RETURN` the return cause (`disposition`, `owner_change`, `catalogue`) and the house operation reference, and the shared chain columns `original_character_revision`, `committed_character_revision` (= original + 1), `level_before/after`, `experience_before/after` (unchanged) and the revision-identity columns the other kinds carry (model: `game_character_wheel_receipts`, migration 0070). Every hireling transaction of H1 to H4, H6 and H7 inserts exactly one such row per owner it changes, in the same transaction as the hireling row change and the one `CharacterRevision` advance of that owner. A house transaction that returns hirelings of one owner writes one `HOUSE_RETURN` receipt for that owner (its `hireling_id` is NULL) and records the returned hirelings in a bounded child table `game_character_hireling_receipt_returns`, keyed `(character_id, hireling_occurrence_id, hireling_id)` with a foreign key to that receipt row, one row per returned hireling, inserted in the same transaction; the child rows of one receipt number at least 1 and at most `HIRE1-RL-01` (10), enforced by a deferred row-count guard, and a child row exists only for a receipt of kind `HOUSE_RETURN` whose hirelings it names (owned by that character). Every other kind names its single hireling in `hireling_id` and has no child rows. HIRE-DATA-1's migration takes the next free number at allocation, adds the tables, their row and chain guards and grants, and re-issues the guard function with the hireling arm appended to the `chain` union and to the revision-one `EXISTS` list, carrying every receipt kind merged by then (the 0073 pattern); a revision advance without a hireling receipt is rejected by the guard.
- **Non-acting writers.** A Store delivery (H1) and the H4 returns act on the owner without a player command. They are not session-fenced; they are fenced by the delivery id and by the house writer's own fence (the disposition operation, the property revision, the catalogue refresh operation) respectively, take the owner's `character_root` lock, and advance the owner's `CharacterRevision` once in their transaction, so the owner's next player command sees the change as stale state. If review finds that an offline owner's `character_root` lock cannot be taken in the house transaction, the alternative is to classify the hireling rows as an independently revised non-Character domain with its own revision, which needs an explicit amendment of DUR-02 and is not assumed here.
- **Resource rows** (registered by HIRE-DATA-1 before implementation, in `docs/contracts/RESOURCE_LIMITS_REGISTRY.json`, owner contract this decision, fixed hard maxima with configurable range equal to the maximum, `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, boundary tests max accepted and max+1 refused):

  | Row | Resource | Maximum | Evidence |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `HIRE1-RL-01` | hireling rows per character, any state | 10 | `products.md` §Hirelings; Canary `GameStore.ItemLimit.HIRELING` and the offer text |
  | `HIRE1-RL-02` | PLACED hirelings per house tile | 1 | Canary `hireling_lamp.lua` ("cannot spawn another hireling here"); a partial unique index on (world, house key, position) where PLACED |
  | `HIRE1-RL-03` | hireling name length | 29 ASCII letters | the character-name rule (`CHARACTER_AUTHORITY_PLATFORM_BOUNDARY` §6.1), as `GUILD0-RL-01` |
  | `HIRE1-RL-04` | unapplied entitlements per kind (name change, sex change, each dress) per character | 10 | derived from `HIRE1-RL-01`: one use per hireling at most |

  HIRE-WIRE-1 registers its own wire rows (§5).

## 4. Delivery contract (blocked)

The Platform Store contract (Oteryn-Platform PR #1625) is pending; its S-1 to S-9 questions are still open with the owner (item 12). This brief therefore defines only what the Game needs from it:
- a signed, idempotent delivery message per character with a delivery id, a product kind (H1) and a payload (name and sex for `HIRELING`, the service for `HIRELING_SKILL`, the dress for `HIRELING_DRESS`);
- a delivery status callback (applied, held, refused with code, including `HIRELING_LIMIT` and the `HIRE1-RL-04` refusal);
- no price, currency or payment data in the Game.

Until the contract is accepted and merged, HIRE-1 children that touch delivery wait. The packets that do not (the hireling record, placement, removal and the house returns, the NPC jobs by owner-grant) can be written against a stub that grants by a test entitlement.

## 5. Packets (proposed)

1. **HIRE-DATA-1** (hard, persistence review): H2, H3 and H4 tables, guards and transactions: the hireling rows, the apprenticeship set and entitlement counts, the placement and removal writer, the H4 returns inside the HOUSE-1b disposition, owner-change and catalogue-refresh transactions, the receipt kind and guard arm, the session fence, `HIRE1-RL-01` to `-04`. 3. **HIRE-JOBS-1** (hard, economy review): Banker, Trader, Steward and Cook per H5 (the Cook is an NPC-0 BUY: fee and dish mint in one atomic transaction), on the accepted bank, stash and trade writers, with the Canary catalogue and fee content.
4. **HIRE-WIRE-1** (protocol review, Reg): a new optional capability (`HIRELING_V1`; number reserved on the protocol registry issue #162 at allocation, never in this brief) that requires capability 3 `NPC_SERVICE_V1`; one state domain listing the owner's hirelings (id, name, sex, outfit, state, house and position when PLACED) and entitlement counts, sent only to the owner; commands place, remove, apply name, apply sex and allocate dress, each with its typed results (§2 codes and the stale-session refusal). Its rows (list entries, at most `HIRE1-RL-01`; encoded list bytes; command payload bytes, with the name at most `HIRE1-RL-03`) are measured and registered by the packet before implementation, as NPC-WIRE-1 did. A client without the capability never receives the domain and its commands are refused as unsupported; unknown enum values fail closed (`PROTOCOL_OTERYN_V1_REGISTRY` rules). A contract candidate of its own under protocol review.
5. **HIRE-STORE-1** (hard, security and economy review): the delivery consumer of H1, **after** HIRE-NPC-1, HIRE-JOBS-1 and HIRE-WIRE-1 have merged and the Platform Store contract is accepted.
Order: HIRE-DATA-1, HIRE-NPC-1, HIRE-JOBS-1, HIRE-WIRE-1, then HIRE-STORE-1. The plan lists HIRE-1 in Wave 5 after HOUSE-1b; nothing here changes that.
